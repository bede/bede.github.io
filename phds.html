Machine learning for plasmid risk assessment from genomic surveillance
Supervisors: Willem van Schaik, Bede Constantinides and Charlene Rodrigues

Plasmids are the main carriers of antimicrobial resistance. They move resistance genes between bacterial lineages, host species and environmental reservoirs. Some plasmids pose much more risk than others, and it is hard to tell which ones matter in which hosts. Current methods look at the genes a plasmid carries and how mobile it is, but mostly ignore the genetic background of the host bacterium. They also tend to perform poorly on the fragmented genome assemblies that genomic surveillance usually produces.

In this project you will build interpretable machine learning models that identify high-risk plasmids and rank them for further investigation. You will test whether taking host genetic background into account improves risk assessments based on resistance gene content, mobility potential and observed host range. You will evaluate the models on well-characterised clinical isolates and measure how reliably plasmid risk can be assessed from genome assemblies, or directly from genomic and metagenomic sequencing reads.

You will develop skills in microbial genomics, bioinformatics and applied machine learning, and build tools that support public health surveillance of antimicrobial resistance.

FAQ

Can international students apply?
No. We can only accept students who are eligible for home (UK) fees. See the home fee eligibility rules.

Can clinicians apply?
Yes, if you are eligible for home fees. The stipend is paid at the standard UKRI rate of £21,805. We can't support clinical salary rates.

Can I apply for more than one project?
Yes. Say clearly in your application which projects you are applying for.

Can I study part-time?
Part-time study is possible, but the total length of funding is limited. Please discuss this with the supervisors before applying.

Are these projects suitable for distance learning?
No. All three projects require you to study in person.
